Kubernetes and Azure Red Hat OpenShift, hands-on

From zero containers to an Azure Red Hat OpenShift (ARO) cluster. Every chapter ends with a lab that you run on your own Azure subscription, and a script that checks your work, stage by stage, and tells you what to fix.

The course is written for people who know basic Azure (the portal, subscriptions, resource groups, az login, virtual networks at a high level) and have never used containers or Kubernetes. About this course describes the goal, the learner profile and what you will be able to do after each part.

How the labs work

You work on a small Linux VM in your own Azure subscription, created in chapter 0. Your own computer only needs the Azure CLI and an SSH client: the scripts that run there exist for PowerShell on Windows and for bash on macOS and Linux. Everything else (Docker, kind, kubectl, the Azure CLI on Linux) is installed on the lab VM, the way a platform team works from a shared jump host.

Use the picker at the top of every page to choose your computer. Command blocks that differ between platforms then show the right variant, and the choice is remembered from page to page. Commands that run on the lab VM are always bash and are labelled as such.

Every lab has the same four parts:

  1. A setup script that prepares what the lab needs.
  2. Tasks written as goals with acceptance criteria. The chapter never gives you a solution to copy and paste.
  3. A validation script that reads your files and the state of your work, and prints [PASS], [FAIL] or [WARN] for each criterion, with a hint for each failure. From chapter 1 onwards it also has a guided mode, which follows you task by task, and an optional deep mode, which adds live tests. Without that option it never changes anything.
  4. A teardown script that removes what the lab created and lists anything still tagged for the course.

From chapter 1 onwards, each task has two hints that you open when you need them, and a broken lab follows the lab: a script puts one fault into your finished work, and you find and repair it. Each chapter also has a lab sheet, which is the lab alone on one page.

Reference solutions are in Reference solutions. Look at them after you have tried, not before.

Cost policy

The course is designed for a subscription with a monthly credit of about 130 EUR and the spending limit on. With the spending limit on, Azure disables the subscription for the rest of the billing period when the credit runs out, and stops its virtual machines. The guardrails below exist so that this never happens to you by accident.

  • The spending limit stays on.
  • Chapter 0 creates a monthly budget with alerts at 50%, 80% and 100% of actual cost, and at 100% of forecast cost.
  • The lab VM shuts down automatically every evening.
  • Every lab has a teardown script, and every teardown lists what is still tagged for the course.
  • Nothing runs overnight unless a chapter says so.
  • ARO only appears in chapters 8 and 9, as short labs that create and delete a cluster in the same session.

Each chapter states its estimated cost in its "Before you start" section, with the source and date of the prices. As a guide, chapters 0 to 2 cost about 1.50 EUR if you do them over three days and then destroy the lab VM, or about 13 EUR a month if you keep the lab VM and use it 2 hours a day (Sweden Central prices, November 2026).

Getting started

  1. Get the course folder on your computer: download the repository that hosts this site as a zip file and extract it, or clone it with Git.
  2. Read chapter 0. It lists what to install on your computer and walks you through the lab setup.
  3. Copy .env.example to .env in the course folder and fill it in. .env holds your settings (prefix, region, budget, your public IP address). It holds nothing secret and is never committed.
  4. Follow the chapters in order. Each chapter builds on the previous one.
